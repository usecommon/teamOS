Team OS · the borders of control

Here are the borders. You decide what crosses.

The system's job is to make every border visible and ask — not to decide for you. It shows the line, tells you what's on the other side, and waits. The one exception: it will not carry you across an irreversible border without your explicit say.

Outer zone · The world — outside your reach Sent email, spent money, deleted data, disclosed information, anything a third party saw. Cross here and it cannot be taken back.
irreversiblethird-partyno undo
◇ Hard border Deny-by-default. Nothing crosses without an allow-policy match or your confirmation — and every crossing is recorded before it happens.
Inner zone · The interior — your free zone Agents draft, read, coordinate, leave trails, emerge. Watched, but not gated. If something's wrong here, you unwind it.
reversibleobservedruns free
● You & your agents Where the work starts. Everything you do here is yours to take back.
free / reversible — no gate, just observed the crossing — you decide the world — irreversible
The rule that draws every border: reversible vs. irreversible. Inside the reversible interior, let it run — you can always take it back. At the irreversible border, the system stops and asks, because there is no taking back. You cross; it never crosses for you.

Who owns the borders

This matters most: the borders are your code, not the vendor's. You can inspect them, move them, and walk out with them.

Team OS — your code, your repo

Owns every border above

  • The gate (payload inspection) — a hook you review before you run it
  • The allow / stop policy — editable files you write
  • The door — exposes only read + propose, by omission
  • The ten ledgers — the record of every crossing
  • The envelope, the breaker, the promotion gate
Claude / the harness — the ground it runs on

Carries, does not decide

  • The runtime the agents execute on
  • The MCP transport under the door
  • A fail-closed backstop if the gate hook ever crashes
  • Not the borders — those come with you on git clone
Exit = clone. Because the borders are files you own, you can leave with everything in your scope. A control surface you can walk out with is, by definition, yours — not the vendor's.
