The product

What Team OS is

A team tool where a member can be a human or an agent. Every person gets their own governed, proactive agent — and agents can be teammates in their own right — doing your recurring work unprompted, keeping every action on the record, and getting better across the whole team as any one agent learns.

The core unit is the per-person agent. Team OS isn't one shared assistant the group logs into — it's one agent per member, each compiled from that member's own work, speaking in their voice, acting within their scope. And a member need not be human: an agent can hold a slice of the work as a teammate too. The "team" is the network of these members and their agents, not a chatbot with many seats.

Multi-user

A team of agents

Each teammate has their own agent. What one agent proves out can be promoted — through a review gate — into the shared registry, so a second person's agent gets better without that person doing the work. Knowledge transfers; toil doesn't repeat. That cross-person compounding is the whitespace.

Single-user

A team of one

A team of one still works, because a member need not be human. You plus agent members — each a scoped, governed sub-agent owning a slice of your work (the digest agent, the compile agent, the scout) — is the team. Add a second human later and they just add another agent to a team that already existed.

What makes it a team, not just personal agents

Learning compounds across people under governance. Sharing rides on protections built before any data mixes: each principal's store is encrypted with their own key, reads are self-ledgered to the owner, and dashboards are per-skill — never a leaderboard of people.

What it is not

Not a memory/RAG product, not a shared chatbot, not a dashboard that ranks people. Memory and corpus are how it knows; the initiative is what it is. If it ever ships recall without initiative, it has drifted into the crowded category and lost the point.

Choose one

Two ways to run on a subscription

Both use your Claude subscription for inference — no API key. They differ in where the loops run, which decides how much of the work your own gate and ledger can see.

A · Local scheduled tasks

Full trust
Recommended

Claude Code Desktop, signed in with your subscription, runs the loops headless on your own always-on machine as local scheduled tasks.

  • Your hooks fire locally — every action is gated and ledgered.
  • Transcripts on your disk; L1/L2/L8 records complete.
  • What defines full trust is that the hooks run here — not who bills the tokens. A subscription qualifies.
  • Needs a machine that stays on for scheduled runs.

B · Cloud scheduled tasks

Reduced trust
No machine required

Loops run as Cowork Scheduled Tasks / Routines on Anthropic's cloud, on your subscription — no daemon, no always-on box.

  • Your hooks do not demonstrably run there; only Team OS door actions are gated and recorded.
  • Scheduled prompts may use the Team OS door only — no third-party connectors (enforced by the loop-prompt linter before install).
  • Convenience for phone-only / no-machine use; see Trust & disclosure before choosing it.

You can start on B and upgrade to A later — installing the CLI is detected and the posture upgrade is itself ledgered.

Capabilities

What it does

Compiler · FK4

Compiles your job

Turns your history into a map of recurring work plus a first draft "built from your last N." Injected/imperative content is quarantined, never compiled.

Brief · FK5

Day One Brief

An unrequested digest + ✓/✗ job draft + first deliverable, delivered to your Slack DM. Your ✓/✗ reactions are recorded as signal.

Loops · FK6

Works while you don't

Daily digest and recurring deliverables drafted on schedule as shadow drafts, scored over time. Resumes cleanly if the machine restarts mid-run.

Gate · FK2

Every action inspected

A hook looks inside each action — commands, paths, payloads — and permits, asks, or blocks. Exfiltration attempts are caught and logged.

Ledgers · FK2

On the record

Hash-chained, append-only logs of what ran, why it was permitted, and every memory write's lineage. Tampering breaks the chain.

Envelope · FK6b

Autonomy you earn

Permissions widen slowly on your own approvals and narrow instantly on a rejection. Every change is reversible and logged.

Door · FK6c

Reach it from any client

Read your corpus and check what the loops produced from any MCP client. Writes only ever propose — into an inbox, never straight to the corpus.

Deletion · FK3

Real forgetting

A deletion walks the lineage graph and tombstones every derived copy; a promoted skill is flagged for review, never silently kept.

Privacy · FK7-pre

Your store, your key

Your personal store is encrypted with a key that never enters the shared repo. No one else can read it; any read lands in your own access feed.

Install

Setup & onboarding

The steps below are the hand-assembled path. Mode A (local) uses all of them; Mode B (cloud) uses 1–5, then registers schedules in one guided chat instead of 8.

  1. Check prerequisites

    Python 3.10+, git, and Claude Code signed in with your subscription. For Mode A, a machine that stays on.

  2. Clone & install

    git clone https://github.com/usecommon/teamOS.git
cd teamOS
python3 -m pip install -r requirements.txt
make test          # 140 tests + gates should pass

  3. Review the hooks — your security control

    The gate hook runs with hook privilege on your machine, so you verify it before trusting it. Read hooks/gate.py (~190 lines, ~15 minutes) against its spec hooks/BEHAVIOR.md. Confirm it makes no network calls and uses no language model. Proceed only if the code matches the spec.

  4. Activate the gate

    Merge hooks/settings-fragment.json into .claude/settings.json so every tool call is gated and ledgered, then confirm:

    python3 scripts/teamos.py doctor      # "gate activated: yes"

  5. Configure sources & delivery

    In config/connectors.json: set inference.mode to owned (your local subscription session counts), add your sources with enabled:false, and set the Slack DM target to your own Slack user id. Nothing is read until you toggle a source on. See Connecting sources.

  6. Set your encryption key — outside the repo

    Your personal store is encrypted; the key must never be committable.

    mkdir -p ~/.teamos/keys      # NOT inside the cloned repo

    The tool refuses a key directory inside the repo by construction.

  7. Compile your footprint

    python3 scripts/teamos.py compile \
    --footprint <dir-of-your-artifacts> \
    --cc ~/.claude --out stores

    You get a job graph, skills imported from your Claude Code commands, and a profile from your CLAUDE.md. Raw ingest is not retained.

  8. See the Brief, then schedule the loops

    python3 scripts/teamos.py brief --out stores   # dry-run render
python3 scripts/teamos.py loop  --out stores   # one pass

    Mode A: register the loop as a local scheduled task on your always-on machine. Mode B: open Claude and say "set up my Team OS schedules" — it reads the desired set (plugin/schedule-templates/) and creates them via the platform's own scheduling tools; a daily reconciliation task repairs any drift.

Connecting sources

Team OS keeps three credential worlds separate. Connecting a source to Claude does not connect it to Team OS, and vice versa — different owners, different revocation.

How to connect, in practice

Team OS sources (what it reads). Add an entry to config/connectors.json with a credential_domain of teamos_plane_a, then toggle it on when you're ready:

{
  "source_id": "my-team-slack",
  "type": "slack",
  "workspace": "myteam.slack.com",
  "credential_domain": "teamos_plane_a",
  "enabled": false          // opt-in: read nothing until true
}

DMs & email are a separate opt-in — enabling a source does not enable its DMs until you set dm_email:true. Every ingested item carries its workspace as provenance.

The optional allowlist (empty by default) holds any rule you or your org want enforced. Empty means no constraint; populated means only listed sources are read, even if enabled. It ships empty of opinion.

Slack DM for the Brief. Set the notification channel target to your authenticated Slack user id — the only identity whose ✓/✗ reactions count.

Note for Mode B: connectors enabled on your Claude account are reachable to cloud sessions and are ungoverned by Team OS. Keep those sessions on a dedicated account with no other connectors enabled — see below.

Read before Mode B

Trust & disclosure

Mode A (local) is fully gated and ledgered. Mode B (cloud) trades some of that for not needing a machine — and that trade is stated plainly, not buried.

Cloud-mode disclosure

"Loops run on Anthropic's cloud; only their Team OS actions are gated and recorded by Team OS; the session itself is governed by Anthropic, not by your hooks — and other connectors enabled on your Claude account may be reachable to the session and are ungoverned by Team OS."

Cheap mitigations (offered in setup)

Run Mode B on a dedicated Claude account with no other connectors enabled, and let the door serve summary-tier views to cloud sessions. Together these remove both the connectors an injection could invoke and the full-content payloads it could carry.

The loop-prompt linter enforces the door-only fence before a schedule is installed: Team OS tools only, no third-party connector names, no raw URLs.

Guarantees

Data & privacy

Files are the record

Corpus, skills, and profile are plain files in git. Databases and indexes are derived and rebuildable — never the source of truth.

Opt-in by default

Every source ships off. Nothing is read until you toggle it, and each toggle is ledgered. DMs and email are a second, separate opt-in.

Encrypted & personal

Your personal store is encrypted with your key, which never enters the shared repo. A clone gives you your own decryptable scope — never anyone else's.

Deletion that propagates

A deletion walks the lineage graph and reaches every derived copy within the deadline; third-party erasure enters the same way.

No individuation

Dashboards render per-skill, never per-person-comparative. There is no leaderboard of people — enforced in code, not just policy.

Untrusted stays inert

Content from your sources can supply values, never plan structure. It can't add a step, change a recipient, or trigger a tool without your confirmation.
