The system in one picture — and where UI earns its place.

Team OS acts on your behalf without being asked. That inverts the usual question: the interface isn't how you drive it, it's how you review and steer something already in motion. This maps the whole system, then the handful of moments where a human surface genuinely helps — each one grounded in a decision the system already makes.

The whole system, as one flow

Everything an agent does crosses the same checkpoint and lands on the same record.

The principal doesn't operate the loop — they seed it and steer it. Work flows left to right: the agent proposes, the gate inspects the payload, and the result forks three ways — recorded, enacted, surfaced — then returns to the human as something to review. A safety rail overrides the whole flow; two connection planes feed it.

The principle that decides everything

Initiative before interface. UI reviews and steers — it never becomes the reason the agent acts.

Because the agent already works unprompted, UI concentrates at the decision moments: a proposal that needs a yes, a draft that wants a ✓, a dial you might turn, a record you want to check, a source you're choosing to open. Most of these already exist as CLI commands — the opportunity is to give the moment a human face, in a surface you already have.

The non-obvious call

The most valuable UI is usually not a dashboard. §7.5's channel symmetry says: reply where you were invoked, at native formality. A proposal-approval card in Slack beats a web console you have to remember to open — the console is the power surface, the DM is the daily one. Build the chat card first.

What UI must never be

Six lines no surface may cross.

These aren't style preferences — they're system invariants. A UI that breaks one isn't a worse design; it's a different, unsafe product.

No individuation surfaceRender per-skill, never per-person-comparative. The individual-telemetry view is structurally absent — not hidden, not permissioned. A "team leaderboard" is the canonical anti-pattern.
Initiative before interfaceThe agent acts because loops fire, not because someone opened a screen. If a surface becomes the trigger for work, the identity claim has inverted.
Opt-in, per source, ledgeredNothing reads DMs or email until that source's toggle is on, and every toggle writes to the record. Consent is a control, not a checkbox in a wizard.
Access is self-ledgeredLooking at a principal's data lands in that principal's own feed. There is no unobserved view of someone else's record.
Presence is non-suppressibleMenu-bar presence, "I'm active" notices, and notify-on-every-surface for new device / connector / policy changes — plus duress affordances. The IPV/tech-abuse defense lives in the UI.
Channel symmetryReply in the surface you were invoked from, at its native formality. Don't drag a Slack interaction into an email, or a quick ✓ into a form.
